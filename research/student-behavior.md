## 4. Behavioral biases relevant to product design

| Bias | Evidence strength | How it plausibly shows up in student housing | Design relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Commuting paradox / commute underweighting | Strong (Stutzer & Frey 2008); student GPA evidence moderate (Milan 2024) | Trading a cheaper/nicer unit for a longer commute, judged in September but lived in January | Make commute concrete per the student's own weekly schedule, including winter and late-night returns |
| Focusing illusion | Strong (Schkade & Kahneman 1998) | Salient amenities (rooftop, gym, new finishes) dominate the tour; routine factors (noise, light, walk at 11pm, laundry) get ignored | Ask about routines first; show a "typical Tuesday" rather than amenity lists |
| Choice overload | Weak on average (meta ≈ 0), but moderated by preference uncertainty | First-time renters with unclear preferences + many listings | Don't assume fewer options always helps; help students *form* preferences, then filter |
| Shrouded / hidden costs | Regulatory evidence (Ann Arbor 2025/2026 fee laws) | Headline rent ≠ total cost (utilities, parking, fees, furniture, summer months on 12-mo lease) | Total-cost-of-occupancy per person per month |
| Present bias / scarcity pressure | Pressure documented; mechanism unverified | Signing before needs (roommates, internships, study abroad) are known | Show legal timeline and actual risk of waiting |
| Herding / peer info | Moderate (Riel 2025; Ferreira & Wong 2020) | "Everyone signs now," "my friends live on State St" | Transparent, non-peer info can reduce class gaps in information |
| Attribute non-attendance | Not verified for this domain | Ignoring lease clauses (joint liability, sublet rules) | Hypothesis to test in user interviews |

## 7. Consequences for product design (evidence-linked, not a product proposal)

1. **Timing information is a high-value, underexploited gap (RSI, M).** The law changed in 2021 and again in March 2024; stale numbers (150-day, 240-day) still circulate; loopholes have been reported. A tool that states "the earliest your landlord can legally lease your current unit to someone else is ~day 210" directly counters scarcity pressure.
2. **Total cost, not rent (RSI, M).** Fee laws (2025/2026) and utility variation in older houses mean the headline number misleads.
3. **Routine-based commute modeling (RSI, M–H).** Commute is systematically underweighted; Ann Arbor adds winter (~121 freezing days) and late-night study (UgLi 24/7) dimensions. Free Blue Bus + free TheRide with MCard means transit cost is ~$0 but *time and frequency* differ sharply (e.g., Bursley-Baits after 9:45pm).
4. **Roommate decisions are made before roommate fit is known (H, L–M).** Joint leases signed early lock in untested groups; evidence on prevalence is thin.
5. **Equity of information (VF, M).** Informal networks advantage some students (Riel 2025); a transparent tool can level this.

## 8. Technical implications

- **Transit data:** AAATA (TheRide) publishes GTFS (expected; verify). UM Blue Bus has a public real-time feed/app (MBus); availability of static GTFS for Blue Bus needs verification. Plan for time-of-day + weekend + snow-route variants.
- **Routing:** walking/biking times via OSM-based routing (e.g., OSRM/Valhalla) with a winter penalty factor (assumption to calibrate).
- **Lease-law engine:** a simple date calculator (lease start → day 180, 210, +30 response) with "applies only in city limits, leases > 8 months" guards; must cite §8:530 and the 2026 tenant booklet.
- **Cost model:** rent + fees + estimated utilities (by building type/age; Green Rental disclosures if available) + months unoccupied (summer) – sublet expectation.
- **Preference elicitation:** schedule-based input (class locations by day, late-night study, work, gym) rather than demographic input — consistent with challenge rules.
- **Data provenance:** every number shown should carry source + date because Ann Arbor rules change frequently (2021, 2022, 2024, 2025, 2026).
